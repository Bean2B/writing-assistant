# Opening brief

Use the user's premise and constraints first. Make the remaining creative decisions in the language of the book. A few concrete sentences are enough to begin drafting.

## The opening situation

Choose a protagonist who wants something before the plot calls on them. Place them in a particular livelihood, time and place. Pick one local circumstance that affects what they can do today. Who has a good reason to oppose the first thing they try? Sketch the choice that puts the two interests into conflict.

Give the protagonist a way to act and a meaningful limit. For cultivation fiction, decide what cultivation can change for this person, how the first gain is earned and where it would still fail. An unusual advantage is optional. For another genre, choose a capacity or resource that belongs in its world.

## Scale and stopping point

- Keep the **whole story**, the **current volume**, and **this assignment** distinct. A brief such as “单卷，约二十万字、六十章” gives a sense of scale. Keep both estimates approximate in the saved brief. Let the central conflict find its ending, then update the estimates as needed. Follow an explicitly requested delivery limit such as “写完第十章就停”.
- For a new long Chinese serial, use **about 2,000,000 body Han characters for the whole book** as the starting estimate unless the user gives another target. The story can finish earlier or run longer as its central conflict develops.
- Use **60–100 chapters for the opening volume** and **100–200 for a later volume** as planning references. A large arc can span two volumes, roughly **200–400 chapters together**. If a decisive turn lands ten or twenty chapters earlier or later than planned, the volume break can move with it.
- **2,000–4,000 body Han characters** is a useful starting range for Chinese long-form chapters. A short aftermath may close on a single gesture. A confrontation may need more space for the other person's reply. Differences of 500–1,000 characters between chapters can arise naturally. Let each chapter run shorter or longer as needed. Count body text when reporting length, excluding the title, punctuation and standalone section labels.

## Current volume

Sketch the opening choice, a few consequential turns and a possible closing change. What becomes possible for the protagonist by then, and what makes walking away harder? Name one relationship or part of the surrounding world that could change with them. Leave room for failure, changed desires and threads whose time has yet to come. Develop the next scene in detail and leave later volumes open.

Keep the resulting decisions short. Mark the user's requirements, the author's provisional choices and any consequential open question. Synthesize any supplied setting sheets or instructions here in the book's own terms. Save the filled brief as an author-visible setting record named `opening-brief` and revise it when a decision changes. Only documentary or literary evidence that needs a source check belongs in a separate research record or source file.
